Q1 · Where my agent sits on the map

(a) My quadrant

Full-Court Press sits in AI alone, with checks (bottom left).

(b) The two questions

Is there a right answer? Yes. The agent’s job is to relay every new item in a Google News feed for “WNBA” to the league comms inbox, accurately and once. Each run can be checked as right or wrong. In my first run at 11:28 PM on 18 October, the headline, publish date and link in the email matched the feed item exactly, which shows a correct relay. The failures are just as clear: of 37 runs, 27 were throttled by Email by Zapier’s sending limit and never delivered, three stories triggered twice (the repeat runs were throttled), and some “new” items were weeks old. Every success and failure can be checked against Zap history, which is what work with a right answer looks like.

Must people and AI do it together? No, either can do it alone. All 37 runs finished with no human input during the run. A person could also do the job alone by checking Google News every hour. Nothing in the process requires a person’s judgment before a run can finish.

(c) The person in the loop

The agent may fetch and forward stories alone, but a person stays at the gate in two places. First, the league comms manager reads alerts at 10 AM daily and decides whether the league responds. That’s a judgment the agent never makes. The “Dirty Play” story about a player’s conduct is an example: relaying it had a right answer, while deciding whether to respond doesn’t. Second, someone audits Zap history weekly against the feed, checking for throttled runs, duplicates, outdated stories and spam such as the fake “WATCH LIVE NOW” streaming pages, two of which came through as successful runs. This check matters because the agent doesn’t report its own failures: 27 missed alerts produced no warning in the inbox. Zapier did block a broken feed URL with a 404 email when I tried to publish it, but nothing flags a feed that stops producing items once it’s live.

The agent would move to A person decides, AI alongside if it drafted responses or rated each story’s risk, since those have no single right answer. It would move to A person leads, AI inside if it posted holding statements only after the comms manager approved them.

Side note · Operations: Escalations of Disputes

Where I agree

Most of my group's placements. For example, Insurance: Check completeness of claims document belongs in AI alone, with checks. Like my Full-Court Press agent, it has a right answer: a document is either complete or it isn't, so a person only needs to spot-check the result.

Question 1 · Is there a right answer? Same answer

No single right answer. The outcome depends on fairness, the relationship with the customer or supplier, and how much the company is willing to concede. None of that is a fact AI can verify.

Question 2 · Together, or can either do it alone? Different answer

My groupWork people and AI must do together: agents run the process, and a person judges at set points inside it.
MeWork either can do alone. In practice, a manager handles it alone.

Why I would move it

  • An escalated dispute is usually a single, high-stakes case rather than a high-volume process.
  • A manager can resolve it without AI.
  • AI prepares the case: it summarizes the history, finds similar past cases and the relevant policy, and drafts two or three settlement options.
  • The person makes the decision and is accountable for it.
  • This matches the bottom-right example in the Week 7 deck: a banker plans a hard negotiation while AI drafts the options.

When my group would be right

If disputes arrived in large numbers, agents could triage them, gather evidence and propose resolutions, and a person would judge only the contested ones. The work would then be a process people and AI run together, and Agents run it, a person judges would fit.

So our disagreement is really about volume and design. For a single escalated case, a person decides, with AI alongside.

My agent card | Full-Court Press | finished 18 October 2026, 11:28 PM
1. The job: Emails each new WNBA news story to the league office’s communications manager (role-played by me) so no story is missed before media calls
2. The trigger: A new item in a Google News RSS search for “WNBA” (RSS by Zapier, New Item in Feed)

Setup and runs

In the first 28 minutes, 37 runs fired: 10 sent and 27 were throttled by Email by Zapier’s sending limit and never delivered. Three stories triggered twice (each repeat run was throttled, so none reached the inbox twice), and some ‘new’ items were weeks old (the oldest from 26 August), because Zapier triggers on items new to it, not newly published.

Errors I caught (not planned)

How the scope changed

  1. League-wide. I started with every story from a Google News search for ‘WNBA’.
  2. One team. I narrowed the alerts to the Golden State Valkyries, the team in the Finals. Only the alert email’s wording changed (“New story mentioning the Golden State Valkyries”). The feed itself stayed the league-wide ‘WNBA’ search, as every run in Zap history shows, so league-wide stories still came through. At 11:47 PM I changed the wording back to “New WNBA story”.
  3. A different team, as a test. I switched to a Dallas Wings search to see whether a team feed would forward everything about that team, betting news included.
  4. Back to league-wide. The Wings feed failed, so I returned to the ‘WNBA’ search and reframed the job as league comms.
